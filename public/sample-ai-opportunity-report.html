Priority order

The first five moves.

This is the part designed to calm the owner down. Not everything has to happen at once. The first few wins create the foundation.

1

Missed-call rescue agent Front Desk

Answer after-hours calls, qualify intent, and route urgent leads before they shop around.

Why first

The business already gets enough calls. The fastest upside is catching the ones nobody answers.

Payoff

$3,000/mo potential recovered revenue

Setup time

Same-day setup

2

Instant text-back follow-up Sales

Reply to new inquiries in seconds, capture the reason for contact, and keep the lead warm.

Why second

Speed-to-lead compounds everything else and works before a CRM rebuild is finished.

Payoff

Hours saved every week

Setup time

1 short workflow

3

Simple CRM pipeline Operations

Move leads from inboxes and sticky notes into visible stages with owner, next step, and value.

Why third

It is a bigger lift, but it prevents every future automation from becoming another disconnected tool.

Payoff

$3k-$5k implementation value

Setup time

3-5 day build

4

Review request automation Marketing

Why fourth

Payoff

More reviews without more admin

Setup time

1 short workflow

4-day quick win plan

What the first implementation can look like.

The assessment is not a theory document. It turns into a focused rollout that can start with one high-payoff workflow.

Day 1

Confirm call types, urgency rules, business hours, routing, and intake fields.

Day 2

Build the missed-call and text-back flow, then test it against real customer scenarios.

Day 3

Connect notifications, lead capture, and handoff steps so the owner sees every new opportunity.

Day 4

Launch, monitor conversations, adjust the script, and decide whether to build the next priority.

Sample report only. The real assessment uses the business owner's answers, current tools, call volume, lead sources, follow-up process, and operational constraints to rank the roadmap.
